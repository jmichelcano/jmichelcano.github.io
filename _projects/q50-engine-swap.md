## The problem

In December 2022 I bought a 2020 Infiniti Q50 with about 24,700 miles from a salvage auction. It was listed as run-and-drive. About a mile into driving it, the engine seized.

## What I did

Instead of rebuilding the seized engine, I found a donor: another wrecked Q50 with the same 3.0L twin-turbo V6. Over the next eight months I did the swap myself in my garage:

- Stripped the front end down to the radiator support and tagged connectors and hoses before disconnecting them.
- Pulled the original engine and transmission.
- Prepared the donor engine and installed it, following the factory service manual's procedures and torque specs.
- Reconnected the harnesses, cooling, and intake, then reassembled the front end.

## Result

The car runs and drives on the donor engine, a 3.0L VR30DDTT twin-turbo V6 driving all four wheels.

The hardest part wasn't any single big job. It was the small details: keeping track of every fastener and torquing each one to spec. A few problems showed up after the swap: a CV axle failed, the shifter linkage worked loose, and putting off an alignment cost me a full set of tires. I diagnosed and fixed each one, and learned to treat the alignment as part of the job.
