## The challenge

A long-duration lunar habitat has to produce some of its own food with limited power, volume, and crew time. Our team proposed a compact galley that grows mushrooms in a closed loop.

We started from a list of requirements. The unit had to:

- fit within the volume limit
- support one crop channel
- keep light, temperature, moisture, and containment stable
- track water and energy use
- stay serviceable in lunar dust, low gravity, and space gloves
- keep food clean and safe
- be repeatable and maintainable

## What we designed

The concept was a team effort. The design work covered:

- **Power estimates** for lighting and control electronics.
- **Nutrient estimates** for the food the mushrooms produce.
- **System layout**: vertical growing towers, a removable tray, and integrated grow lighting.
- **Energy use and efficiency** of the whole unit.
- **Ease of use and transport**, so a crew could operate it and move it as a single unit.

The full-size galley is 1.0 m long, 1.0 m tall, and 0.5 m wide.

## My part

On a team of five, I built the CAD model of the full system and then built the working prototype at ⅓ scale: 13.12 × 13.12 × 6.56 in, with 10.5 in growing towers. I also supplied the Arduino and electronics that bring it to life:

- a temperature and humidity sensor, with a buzzer that alerts when conditions drift
- LED grow lights
- an LCD and keypad for choosing a grow profile, mushrooms or lettuce

## Result

We took **first place** at the 2025 Innovation Works Challenge, sponsored by Lockheed Martin Skunk Works.
